# Variant A — Father's Last Egg

Concept: 2 a.m. at a Jakarta warung, father splits his last fried egg over
my rice and calls it a feast; the smallest share becomes the richest meal.
Intergenerational care motif, concrete (warung, fried egg, rice, 2 a.m.),
turn: the smallest share becomes the richest meal.

## Final sentence (v8) — 181 characters

> At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father split his last fried egg over my rice, the yolk running gold, and called it a feast — the smallest share that ever made me rich.

## Version history

- **v1 — 144 chars:** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father split his last fried egg over my rice and called it a feast, and I have never eaten better." The core scene is there, but the ending ("I have never eaten better") is generic praise any entrant could write, and there is no sensory detail.
- **v2 — 138 chars:** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father cracked his last fried egg over my rice, the yolk still runny, and called it a feast." Adds the yolk's texture, but "cracked" is weaker than "split" (splitting is the sharing act) and the sentence ends flat, with no turn.
- **v3 — 137 chars:** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father split his last fried egg over my rice, the yolk running gold, and called it a feast." "The yolk running gold" is the strongest image so far, but the sentence still stops at the feast — no landing, no meaning flip.
- **v4 — 155 chars:** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father halved his last fried egg onto my rice and called it a feast, and I have never tasted anything richer." Adds a closing claim, but "halved" is clumsier than "split" and "I have never tasted anything richer" is again generic praise, not an image.
- **v5 — 189 chars:** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father split his last fried egg over my rice and called it a feast, and the yolk running gold across the rice is still the richest meal I know." First version with a real turn — the yolk becomes the richest meal — but the ending sprawls ("is still the richest meal I know") and repeats the feast idea instead of landing.
- **v6 — 170 chars:** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father split his last fried egg over my rice, the yolk spilling gold, and called it a feast — the smallest share I ever ate." The em-dash turn arrives: the smallest share. But "the smallest share I ever ate" only restates the split; it does not say why the meal mattered.
- **v7 — 180 chars:** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father split his last fried egg over my rice, the yolk running gold, and called it a feast — the smallest meal that ever made me rich." "Made me rich" is the turn that flips scarcity into wealth. "Meal" is slightly off, though — the point is the *share*, what he gave up.
- **v8 — 181 chars (FINAL):** "At two in the morning in a Jakarta warung, my father split his last fried egg over my rice, the yolk running gold, and called it a feast — the smallest share that ever made me rich." "Share" names exactly what the father gave away, and "made me rich" completes the flip: the least food, the most wealth. Tightest wording of the turn, best image ("yolk running gold"), fullest use of the byte budget.

## Why v8 wins on the 5 judge criteria

1. **On subject:** every word is the best meal ever had — the warung, the egg, the rice, the father's feast.
2. **Concrete:** named place (Jakarta warung), named dish (fried egg over rice), a texture and a colour (yolk running gold), one specific moment (two in the morning).
3. **True to one person:** a father giving away his last egg is a memory only one person could write; no stock line says "the smallest share that ever made me rich."
4. **One clean sentence, reads aloud well:** a single flowing sentence, no cliche ("delicious", "best ever"), fresh wording throughout. "Two in the morning" is spelled out so the sentence has exactly one period.
5. **Lands with an ending that stays:** the em-dash turn — the smallest share made me rich — inverts poverty into wealth in seven words and is the line a reader remembers after one reading.
